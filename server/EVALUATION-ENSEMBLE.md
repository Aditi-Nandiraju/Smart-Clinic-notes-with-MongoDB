## B. Brand names the lexicon has never seen

53 of 160 brand names were removed from the lexicon. The tagger was trained only on notes that never contain them. Test: 85 mentions of those brands in 80 unseen notes.

| | Mentions found | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Rules (reduced lexicon) | 8 | 9.4% |
| Rules + ML tagger (trained on 1206 notes without these brands) | 78 | 91.8% |

A mention "found" by the tagger is either mapped to a drug (by fuzzy match, 8 cases) or offered to the doctor as an unmapped medicine to map in one click. Rules find a few only through fuzzy matching to a similar known brand.

Whole test set with the reduced lexicon — medications F1: rules 94.2%, rules + ML 94.2% (unmapped mentions do not count as found here, because they still need the doctor to say which drug they are).

## C. Learning vocabulary from corrections

Starting from the reduced lexicon, notes arrive in batches of 50. After each batch the simulated doctor corrects the notes and every brand they map is saved to the vocabulary (as `POST /api/encounters/:id/verify` does with `learn: true`). Medication recall on each new batch, before that batch's corrections:

| Batch | Rules: recall | Rules: aliases learned | Rules + ML: recall | Rules + ML: aliases learned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 92.6% | 7 | 92.6% | 7 |
| 2 | 95.1% | 12 | 95.1% | 12 |
| 3 | 95.6% | 16 | 95.6% | 16 |
| 4 | 100.0% | 16 | 100.0% | 16 |
| 5 | 95.8% | 18 | 95.8% | 18 |
| 6 | 98.8% | 19 | 98.8% | 19 |
| 7 | 100.0% | 20 | 100.0% | 20 |
| 8 | 99.0% | 20 | 99.0% | 20 |

Both arms learn the same aliases (the doctor corrects every note either way), so recall recovers at the same rate. The difference is the doctor's effort: with the ML tagger, 18 of 20 new brands were already highlighted as unmapped suggestions and needed only a pick from a list; with rules only, all 20 had to be found in the text and selected by hand.

## D. Confidence-based review triage

Items with confidence below 0.85, or with a fuzzy match, conflict, missing dose or unusual value, are flagged "check"; the rest are auto-accepted (still visible, but the doctor can skip them). 400 unseen generated notes, rules + ML:

| Measure | Generated notes (400) | Hand-written edge + blind (32) |
|---|---|---|
| Items auto-accepted | 2606 of 2666 (97.7%) | 158 of 173 (91.3%) |
| Auto-accepted items the doctor kept unchanged | 100.0% | 98.7% |
| Flagged items the doctor kept unchanged | 80.0% | 86.7% |
| Extractor errors that were flagged | 12 of 12 (100.0%) | 2 of 4 (50.0%) |
| Notes with nothing to check | 355 of 400 | |

"Errors" here are wrong items the extractor produced (an item the doctor changed or removed); items it missed entirely cannot be flagged and are covered by recall in section A.

The live system measures the same numbers on every real verification (`review.autoPrecision` on the encounter) together with the review time in seconds, shown on the Analytics page.

## Limits

- The generated notes come from the same templates as the training notes, so section A's generated-set numbers are optimistic; the hand-written edge and blind sets are the better estimate, and they are small (20 and 12 notes).
- The LLM column was not measured here. The LLM code paths were tested against scripts/mockOllama.js, a heuristic stand-in, which says nothing about real model quality. Run `node scripts/evaluateEnsemble.js --llm` with Ollama running to fill it in.
- The "not recorded in notes" rule list in the assistant was written while looking at section E's questions, so 100% there is optimistic; a fresh set of out-of-scope questions is the honest test.
- The simulated doctor in C and D is perfect; real reviewers disagree with each other (see scripts/agreement.js and docs/ANNOTATION_GUIDE.md for measuring that).
